# Building Water-Based Fire Sprinkler Systems — Assessment and Estimator Requirements

**Status:** Implemented POC direction; still draft for BNM3 technical and estimating launch review
**Current objective:** Review the implemented intake and planning-estimator boundary before operational use or pricing expansion.
**Implementation boundary:** The repository implements the user-approved POC shape. This document does not approve production use, a price book, a formal quotation, engineering conclusions, or technical/estimating policy on its own.

## Confirmed product direction

- Sprinkler assessment and estimator submissions will use the same lead dashboard, qualification review, call record, activity history, status, and site-inspection process as the existing LPG workflow.
- The service and questionnaire differ; the downstream lead-management journey does not become a separate dashboard.
- The public assessment should contain five grouped project questions, plus a safety screen and contact/consent step.
- The budget estimator is a single page and uses dimensions and floor count to produce a clearly labeled planning count and initial head-only allowance. The user approved ₱5,000 per planning head for this POC; other system costs remain excluded.

## 1. Intended product outcome

The assessment should turn nontechnical customer answers into a structured project brief for BNM3. It may identify missing information and recommend one of these next steps:

1. **Technical review** — a qualified BNM3 reviewer can continue from the submitted information.
2. **More information required** — the customer can provide a drawing, test record, measurement, or clarification before review.
3. **Site inspection required** — site conditions, existing-system risk, or project complexity make remote assessment unsuitable.
4. **Urgent human assistance** — a reported active fire, discharge, major leak, damaged system, or disabled required protection exits the normal assessment.
5. **Indicative budget range** — allowed only for BNM3-approved, calibrated project classes with complete minimum inputs and no suppression rule.

An online result is not a hydraulic calculation, sprinkler layout, hazard or occupancy classification, code-compliance decision, permit approval, inspection certificate, signed/sealed design, or binding quotation.

## 2. Research basis and authority boundary

- The Philippine Fire Code IRR requires automatic sprinkler systems called for by the occupancy provisions to follow NFPA 13 or NFPA 13R. It also requires plans and specifications to be signed and sealed by a Professional Mechanical Engineer and calls for inspection, testing, maintenance, and records under NFPA 25. This is why the assessment must collect building use, project type, existing records, and water-supply context while leaving standard selection and engineering decisions to qualified reviewers. [BFP Revised IRR of RA 9514 (2019)](https://region10.bfp.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/RA9514-RIRR-rev-2019.pdf)
- BFP plan evaluation reviews plans, design calculations, specifications, and fire-protection provisions before a Fire Safety Evaluation Clearance is issued. A Fire Safety Inspection Certificate follows inspection of the installed condition for the applicable occupancy or business process. The digital assessment must not present either result as granted or guaranteed. [BFP Revised IRR of RA 9514](https://bfp.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/RA9514-RIRR-rev-2019.pdf) and [BFP enhanced application and online-payment guidelines](https://bfp.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/MC-2024-024-GUIDELINES-AND-PROCEDURES-FOR-IMPLEMENTING-AN-ENHANCED-APPLICATION-AND-ONLINE-PAYMENT-SYSTEM-FOR-THE-RELEASING-OF-FIRE-SAFETY-CERTIFICATES-AND-CLEARANCES-USING-BFP-FIRE-SAFETY-AND-INSPECTION-SY.pdf)
- BFP application guidance identifies project identity, location, floor area, storeys, contractor, and complete discipline and fire-protection documents as relevant review material. It also calls for signed/sealed estimates and supporting reports when applicable. These are document-readiness inputs, not facts the customer flow can validate. [BFP Fire Safety Guidelines on Different Types of Occupancy, Volume 2](https://bfp.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Fire-Safety-Guidelines-on-Different-Types-of-Occupancy-Volume-2.pdf)
- NFPA 13 covers sprinkler-system design, installation, and acceptance testing and identifies layouts, water supplies, components, and installation methods as professional design considerations. [NFPA 13 overview](https://www.nfpa.org/product/nfpa-13-standard-for-the-installation-of-sprinkler-systems/p0013code)
- NFPA distinguishes the residential standards from NFPA 13: NFPA 13D applies to one- and two-family dwellings and manufactured homes, while NFPA 13R applies to qualifying low-rise residential occupancies. The applicable standard cannot be selected from a customer label alone and remains subject to the adopted code, BFP/AHJ, and professional review. [NFPA 13D](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/1/3/d/nfpa-13d) and [NFPA codes and standards list](https://www.nfpa.org/for-professionals/codes-and-standards/list-of-codes-and-standards?l=35)
- Related water-based systems may add standpipes and hose systems, stationary fire pumps, private fire-service mains, or dedicated tanks under NFPA 14, 20, 24, and 22 respectively. The assessment should discover whether these components exist or may be involved, not size or select them. [NFPA codes and standards list](https://www.nfpa.org/for-professionals/codes-and-standards/list-of-codes-and-standards?l=35) and [NFPA 20 overview](https://www.nfpa.org/product/nfpa-20-standard/p0020code?order_src=C900)
- NFPA 25 addresses inspection, testing, and maintenance of water-based fire-protection systems and distinguishes deficiencies from impairments. A reported impairment or uncertain required protection must therefore leave normal estimating and receive prompt qualified review. [NFPA water-based ITM certification reference](https://www.nfpa.org/for-professionals/certification/wbitm) and [NFPA discussion of deficiencies and impairments](https://www.nfpa.org/news-blogs-and-articles/blogs/2022/05/13/deficiencies-and-impairments-of-sprinkler-systems?l=41)
- NFPA material shows that sprinkler placement cannot be reduced to one “head per distance” rule. Maximum protection area and spacing depend on sprinkler type, construction, occupancy/hazard, obstructions, and listing. Published NFPA committee material shows a 15 ft (4.6 m) maximum spacing for the cited standard pendent/upright spray light- and ordinary-hazard tables, while coverage still differs: up to 225 ft² (20.9 m²) in qualifying light-hazard cases and 130 ft² (12.1 m²) for ordinary hazard. These are context-specific limits, not a customer-approved layout. [NFPA 13D/13R committee report](https://docinfofiles.nfpa.org/files/AboutTheCodes/13D/13D_AUT_RSS_A2018_FD%20Agenda%20_07-_16.pdf) and [NFPA 13 sprinkler-location principles and tables](https://docinfofiles.nfpa.org/files/AboutTheCodes/13/13_AUT-AAC_A2018_SDAgenda_11_17.pdf)
- Wet versus dry or preaction protection is not selected merely from building type. NFPA material uses whether piping can be reliably maintained above 40°F (4°C), along with other special conditions, as a key input to freeze-protection decisions. The short questionnaire should detect cold storage/freezer or other special-control conditions and refer the final system choice to the designer. [NFPA residential system-type committee material](https://docinfofiles.nfpa.org/files/AboutTheCodes/13R/13R_A2024_AUT_RSS_FD_Agenda_7_22.pdf)

These sources establish intake boundaries, not a project design. The applicable adopted editions, local requirements, authority having jurisdiction, professional responsibility, manufacturer instructions, and actual site conditions must be confirmed for each project.

## 4. Five-question customer assessment

The common path should use plain language, show “Not sure” where safe, and keep the project intake to five grouped questions. The safety screen and contact/consent step do not count toward the five project questions.

### Safety screen — before question 1

Ask whether there is an active fire, uncontrolled sprinkler discharge, major leak, or a required system known or suspected to be out of service. An active emergency stops the form and shows approved emergency wording. A possible impairment creates a priority-review lead without showing an estimate. The form must not provide repair or valve-operation instructions.

### Question 1 — What kind of building is it?

Ask “Is this a commercial building?” and then capture the closest use in the same question:

- Commercial: office, shop/retail, restaurant, hotel, school, healthcare, or other
- Residential: house, apartment/condominium, dormitory, or other
- Warehouse or storage
- Factory or industrial/process
- Mixed use
- Not sure

This answer is a customer description, not an official occupancy or hazard classification. Warehouse/storage, industrial/process, mixed use, healthcare, and “Not sure” route to technical review and do not use a simple head-count factor.

### Question 2 — Is this a new installation?

- New sprinkler installation in a new building
- New sprinkler installation in an existing building
- Extension or alteration of an existing system
- Repair, inspection, or maintenance
- Not sure

Existing-building work asks one conditional yes/no: “Is a sprinkler system already installed?” A reported defect or shutdown invokes the safety/impairment route.

### Question 3 — How many floors are included?

Collect the number of above-ground floors included and basement levels included. Show one short checkbox for “The floor sizes are approximately the same.” Multiple floors become a review reason and add a per-floor/riser planning allowance only after BNM3 approves the estimating rule.

### Question 4 — What are the approximate dimensions?

Collect length and width with metres or feet, or allow total floor area when that is what the customer knows. If floors differ, accept an area for each floor or the total protected area rather than forcing one typical dimension. Calculate and display the interpreted protected area so the customer can correct it.

Dimensions are for preliminary budgeting only. They do not establish protected areas, room-by-room sprinkler placement, omissions, ceiling pockets, or obstruction compliance.

### Question 5 — Which condition best describes the protected area?

- Normal occupied space; no freezer/cold-storage area
- Includes a freezer, cold room, or area that can fall below 4°C
- Includes water-sensitive or high-value areas where special release control may be needed
- Existing system is known to be wet, dry, preaction, or deluge (optional selection)
- Not sure

This answer produces a **system-review signal**, not an automatic system selection. Normal ambient conditions can be shown as “wet-pipe review likely.” Cold/freezer conditions can be shown as “freeze-protection/dry-system review required.” Water-sensitive or controlled-release conditions can be shown as “preaction/special-system review required.” Only a qualified designer can confirm the system type.

### Contact and consent — after question 5

Collect name, mobile number, optional email, city/municipality, barangay, preferred contact method, and consent. This is the same lead-capture boundary used by the existing workflow and is not presented as a sixth technical question.

### Detailed discovery after lead creation

The five-question form intentionally does not collect enough information for design. The following inventory is for dashboard review, the first call, requested documents, or a site inspection—not additional mandatory public-form pages.

#### Immediate safety and impairment verification

Ask whether any of these are happening now:

- Active fire or smoke emergency
- Sprinkler operating or significant uncontrolled water discharge
- Required sprinkler protection known or suspected to be shut down, isolated, severely damaged, frozen, or otherwise unable to operate
- Recent fire, impact, major leak, or unapproved alteration affecting the system
- None of these / not sure

**Branching:** Active emergency exits the assessment to BNM3-approved emergency wording and local emergency services; the application must not offer troubleshooting. A suspected impairment, major damage, or “not sure” exits pricing and creates a prompt human-review route. BNM3 must approve exact wording, notification handling, staffing hours, and escalation ownership before launch.

#### Requested work verification

- New system in a proposed building
- New system in an existing building
- Extension or alteration
- Tenant fit-out or renovation
- Inspection, testing, or preventive maintenance
- Repair of a known issue
- Fire-pump, tank, standpipe, or underground/private-main work
- Design, calculation, permit, or documentation assistance
- Not sure

For existing systems, ask whether the system is operating, whether work is planned or already performed, and whether an inspection report or deficiency notice exists. Never ask the customer to restore a valve, reset equipment, drain piping, or test the system.

#### Project and building context

Collect:

- Project stage: concept, permit/design, construction, renovation, occupied operation, or maintenance
- Building use in the customer's words; include common choices such as residential, office, retail, school, healthcare, hotel/dormitory, warehouse/storage, factory/process, parking, and mixed use
- Whether there are multiple occupancies or tenants
- New or existing building
- Total floor area and approximate area included in this request, with units
- Number of storeys above grade and basement levels
- Number of buildings or detached structures
- Whether the project involves high-rise conditions, large storage, rack storage, high-piled goods, hazardous materials, industrial processes, atriums, concealed spaces, or another unusual condition; “Not sure” is valid
- City/municipality and barangay/general project area

**Boundary:** The interface may repeat the customer's description but must not assign the official occupancy or sprinkler hazard classification. Mixed use, storage/process risks, multiple buildings, high-rise conditions, or material uncertainty suppress pricing and require technical review.

#### Areas and physical conditions

Collect only observable facts:

- Floors, rooms, tenant areas, or zones included
- Approximate protected area per relevant floor or zone when known
- Ceiling form: open, suspended, concealed, sloped, or mixed
- Approximate ceiling/roof heights in bands
- Existing obstructions or dense services at ceiling level
- Storage presence, maximum storage height, rack use, and stored commodity description in the customer's words
- Occupied versus vacant work area
- Working-hour, shutdown, noise, dust, access, security, or tenant restrictions
- Need for scaffolding, lifts, ceiling opening, trenching, core drilling, painting, or restoration, if known

Customers should not be asked to determine sprinkler spacing, design density, pipe diameter, hydraulic remote area, or obstruction compliance.

#### Existing water-based system

Show this stage only for an existing building or system:

- System presence: none, sprinkler only, sprinkler with standpipe/hose, unknown
- System type if known: wet pipe, dry pipe, preaction, deluge, or unknown
- Approximate installation or last major alteration year
- Areas served and any unsprinklered/isolated areas known to the customer
- Existing sprinkler head count only if documented; otherwise ask for an approximate band or “Unknown”
- Known leaks, corrosion, painted/obstructed/damaged sprinklers, missing supports, closed or inaccessible valves, recurring alarms, or other reported deficiency
- Last inspection/test date and whether records are available
- Availability of approved drawings, as-builts, hydraulic nameplate/calculations, permits, certificates, previous reports, and modification records

Any safety-significant condition, undocumented alteration, uncertain system boundary, missing records for material modification, or conflict between answers routes to review/site inspection. A customer observation is not a system condition certification.

#### Water supply and related systems

Ask what is present or planned, allowing “Unknown” throughout:

- Water source: public utility, dedicated/private source, fire-protection tank, combined source, or unknown
- Recent hydrant/flow-test or water-supply data and test date, if available
- Fire pump: none, electric, diesel, other, or unknown
- Jockey pump, controller, and alternate-power/generator context if known
- Dedicated fire tank and approximate capacity only when documented
- Private fire-service main, fire department connection, backflow device, and main control valves
- Standpipe/hose system and number of risers/zones if documented
- Fire-alarm monitoring or waterflow/tamper interface

Unknown or customer-entered water data is useful for discovery but cannot support pump selection, tank sizing, pipe sizing, or hydraulic sufficiency. A new/changed pump, tank, underground main, standpipe, pressure zone, or uncertain water supply suppresses automated pricing.

#### Proposed scope and quantities

Collect customer-known quantities without turning them into a design:

- Areas/floors to protect, extend, renovate, inspect, or repair
- Approximate number of new, relocated, replaced, or affected sprinklers only when known
- Existing plan takeoff or bill of quantities, if available
- Known risers, control-valve assemblies, drains, test connections, fire department connections, alarms/interfaces, pumps, tanks, and underground work included
- Demolition, temporary protection, ceiling work, painting, testing, commissioning, documentation, training, or closeout needs
- Desired schedule and any required phased work or shutdown window

Pipe size/length and fitting quantities should come from reviewed drawings or a BNM3 takeoff, not customer guesses. If the customer supplies a takeoff, label its source and require reviewer confirmation.

#### Documents and optional evidence

The no-upload path must still submit a lead. Ask whether the customer can later provide:

- Architectural floor plans, reflected ceiling plans, and sections
- Fire-protection drawings and specifications
- Approved or signed/sealed plans
- Hydraulic calculations and hydraulic nameplate information
- Water-flow test or utility data
- Existing as-built drawings and system inventory
- Inspection, testing, maintenance, deficiency, or impairment reports
- Fire Safety Evaluation Clearance, Fire Safety Inspection Certificate, permit, or authority correspondence when relevant
- Photos of representative areas, risers, valves, pump/tank, fire department connection, and reported issues

Before uploads are implemented, BNM3 must define accepted formats and size, malware scanning, access control, retention/deletion, download audit, and whether files containing signatures or building-security details require stricter handling.

#### Contact and consent verification

Collect only after the project questions:

- Customer/organization name
- Contact person's name and role
- Mobile number and optional email
- Preferred contact method and time window
- Consent to be contacted about this request
- Exact street address only when needed for site coordination or document review

Do not collect government identification or unrelated personal information. Publish the purpose, retention, access, and deletion rules before accepting real submissions.

## 5. Decision and routing rules

```text
Active fire or uncontrolled discharge
  -> Stop normal assessment
  -> Approved emergency message; no estimate

Known/suspected impairment, major damage, or uncertain safety state
  -> Prompt human triage
  -> No estimate and no customer repair instructions

New system, existing-building retrofit, mixed/high-rise/industrial/storage risk,
multiple buildings, or pump/tank/main/standpipe scope
  -> Qualified technical review
  -> Site inspection when required; no initial automated amount

Existing-system alteration with usable approved/as-built documents,
clear bounded quantities, and no complex trigger
  -> Technical review
  -> Indicative range only if BNM3 later approves that project class

Fixed-scope inspection/testing/maintenance item in an approved service area
  -> Fixed or ranged budget only after scope, exclusions, and price book are approved
```

### Mandatory no-price triggers

Any one of these suppresses an online range unless BNM3 approves a narrower documented exception:

- Active emergency, impairment, fire damage, uncontrolled leak/discharge, or significant known deficiency
- Customer request for compliance confirmation, hydraulic sufficiency, signed/sealed design, certification, or authority approval
- Governing standard, building use, occupancy mix, scope boundary, or water supply cannot be established by a qualified reviewer
- Existing-building retrofit or modification without reviewed documents/site information
- High-rise, warehouse/rack/high-piled storage, industrial/process, hazardous-material, special-hazard, atrium, or other unusual condition
- Multiple buildings, pressure zones, standpipes, tanks, private mains, or fire-department supply arrangements involved or uncertain; a customer-reported existing fire pump is recorded but never assumed adequate
- Existing-system modification without adequate as-builts, calculations, records, or field verification
- Material conflict among answers, drawings, photos, reports, or observed conditions
- Project location, access, shutdown, restoration, or schedule outside approved operating rules
- Estimate exceeds BNM3's future review-value or uncertainty threshold

## 6. Implemented initial-quote model

The POC uses an explicit, versioned rule and the user-directed BNM3 planning rate, never generated or inferred market prices. The stored estimator snapshot retains the inputs, assumptions, exclusions, coverage factors, spacing reference, pricing count, and per-head rate.

### One-page estimator experience

Keep all controls and the live result on one responsive page:

1. Building use: commercial subtype or non-commercial subtype.
2. Work type: new installation, existing-building installation, alteration, or maintenance.
3. Floors included.
4. Length × width for a typical floor, per-floor areas, or total protected area.
5. Environment/system-review condition from assessment question 5.
6. Whether a fire pump is already installed for the building's fire-protection water supply: yes, no, or not sure.

The result panel shows interpreted total area, the pricing head count, the 4.6 m spacing context, a light-hazard comparison count, the initial head-only allowance, fire-pump status, assumptions, exclusions, review status, and an optional contact form. Submitting contact details creates an `estimator` lead; calculating without opting in does not create a lead, matching the existing LPG estimator behavior.

### Planning sprinkler-count research and rule

Do not ask “How many sprinklers are needed per distance?” A rectangular grid can still be changed by walls, rooms, ceiling geometry, obstructions, construction, storage, and sprinkler listing. Use area only for a budget-planning range:

```text
interpreted area per floor = length × width, or customer-provided floor area
planning count per floor = ceiling(area per floor ÷ approved planning coverage factor)
total planning count = sum of each floor's rounded planning count
```

The implemented pricing count uses 12.1 m²/head as a conservative ordinary-hazard planning envelope and rounds every floor separately so floor boundaries are not lost. A 20.9 m²/head light-hazard reference count is shown for context only. Never present either result as a layout, approved hazard classification, or required quantity.

NFPA examples such as 20.9 m² for stated light-hazard conditions and 12.1 m² for ordinary hazard are maximum coverage values under specific requirements. The POC uses the ordinary-hazard figure as a conservative **budget-planning factor**, not a design determination. No amount is shown for warehouse/storage, industrial/process, mixed/unknown use, unusual ceilings, cold/freezer, special-control, existing-building work, alteration/maintenance, more than four floors, or another mandatory-review condition.

### One-page budget calculation

The simple eligible POC path uses:

```text
ceiling(area per floor ÷ 12.1 m²) × number of equal floors
× ₱5,000 per planning sprinkler head
= initial indicative sprinkler-head allowance
```

The current per-head allowance is not presented as including pipe, fittings, supports, valves, pump/controller, tank, private main, standpipe, alarm integration, electrical work, major access/restoration, testing, hydraulic design, professional services, tax, or authority fees. These remain separate reviewed allowances. The estimator explicitly says that multiplying the planning heads by the unit rate does not price a complete compliant system.

## 7. Detailed estimator cost model

### Cost-variable groups

| Group | Candidate inputs | Required source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design and authority work | plan review, survey, calculations, signed/sealed documents, permit/clearance support | Approved service matrix and professional scope |
| Sprinkler coverage work | reviewed area/zone, approved sprinkler quantity/type, reviewed pipe takeoff | Reviewed design or as-built/takeoff |
| Valves and risers | control assemblies, check/backflow devices, drains/tests, risers/zones | Reviewed system inventory/design |
| Water supply | flow testing, pump, controller, tank, underground/private main, fire department connection | Technical design and supplier quotes |
| Detection/integration | waterflow/tamper devices, fire-alarm interface, monitoring coordination | Reviewed interface scope |
| Construction | demolition, supports/bracing, penetrations, ceiling access, lifts/scaffold, trenching, painting, restoration | Site/drawing evidence and unit rules |
| Labor and logistics | crew trade/time, mobilizations, occupied work, phased shutdowns, security/access, travel | Approved labor and service-zone rules |
| Testing and closeout | flushing, hydrostatic/acceptance tests, commissioning, records, training, as-builts | Approved deliverable matrix |
| Commercial adjustments | waste, escalation, contingency, overhead, margin, tax, validity | Owner-approved commercial policy |

### Candidate formula

```text
Reviewed direct materials
+ Reviewed labor
+ Equipment and access
+ Mobilization and travel
+ Testing, documentation, and professional services
+ Approved subcontract/supplier allowances
+ Restoration allowance
= Direct project cost

Direct project cost
+ Approved waste/complexity/escalation allowance
+ Overhead
+ Contingency
+ Margin
+ Applicable tax treatment
= Indicative budget basis
```

Show a rounded low/high range only after calibration establishes defensible uncertainty bands. Do not create a range by applying an arbitrary percentage to incomplete work.

### Candidate first estimable classes

BNM3 should consider, but must explicitly approve, beginning with tightly bounded work such as a standard inspection visit, a defined NFPA 25 service package, or a small documented alteration with reviewed current drawings. New systems, retrofits, and water-supply changes should remain reviewer-produced estimates until enough historical evidence supports a rule set.

### Calibration gate

Before enabling any customer price:

1. Define included/excluded work and units for each eligible class.
2. Create a versioned price book with named owner and approval date.
3. Test at least five representative completed projects per eligible class, including variance reasons.
4. Define acceptable error, minimum margin, maximum auto-range value, and stale-price behavior.
5. Test incomplete, contradictory, out-of-area, and no-price cases.
6. Obtain technical, estimating, privacy, and customer-copy approval.

If rates expire or a required price-book item is unavailable, fail closed to technical review; do not silently reuse a stale or partial range.

## 8. Result screen and staff brief

### Customer result

- Project need and building description, explicitly “based on your answers”
- Areas and related-system scope reported
- Documents/evidence received and still needed
- Outcome: technical review, more information, site inspection, urgent assistance, or eligible indicative range
- Major assumptions and exclusions
- Human-review boundary and next action
- Expected response time only after BNM3 approves an operating SLA

### Staff brief

- Answers with source/provenance and timestamps
- Safety/impairment flags separated from commercial scoring
- Reasons for every review, site-visit, and no-price rule
- Missing/conflicting evidence
- Uploaded-file metadata and access history when uploads exist
- Estimate rule and price-book versions, line-item basis, overrides, reviewer identity, and approval history when pricing exists

No urgency or safety flag may be downgraded automatically because a lead appears commercially attractive.

## 9. Failure, duplicate, and recovery behavior

- A customer can save or safely retry without creating multiple uncontrolled site requests; the implementation must define an idempotency key and visible confirmation reference.
- If submission fails, retain unsent answers locally only with clear disclosure and a safe expiry; never claim receipt until the backend confirms persistence.
- If pricing fails or inputs become invalid, preserve the assessment and route to review without showing a partial number.
- A duplicate or updated submission should link to the existing lead when confidently matched, while retaining an auditable activity rather than silently overwriting prior answers.
- Staff must be able to distinguish customer corrections from reviewer changes.
- Emergency/impairment routing must not depend solely on email delivery; BNM3 must define monitored channels, coverage hours, acknowledgment, and fallback behavior.

## 10. Safety, privacy, and authorization requirements

- Treat contact information, exact addresses, occupancy/use, drawings, hydraulic data, valve/pump/tank locations, photos, reports, and access instructions as sensitive customer and building-security data.
- Collect coarse location before exact address, minimize public form fields, and never expose submissions or documents through predictable links.
- Require authenticated, server-authorized staff access using current backend roles. The approved JWT hardening milestone must be completed before real customer or production use.
- Define upload scanning, MIME/content validation, size limits, encrypted transport/storage, signed download access, audit logs, retention, deletion, backup, and incident handling before enabling documents.
- Do not place sensitive answers, filenames, tokens, or full addresses in ordinary application logs or analytics.
- Use BNM3-approved emergency and impairment wording. The product must not instruct customers to manipulate fire-protection equipment or declare a building safe.
- Keep estimates explicitly indicative and non-binding. Qualified human review remains mandatory for technical feasibility, site conditions, design, code/application decisions, and final quotation.
